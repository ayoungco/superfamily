The Lego adventurers jungle theme is likely inspired by what Native American culture 

The 1999 **Lego Adventurers Jungle subtheme** (where Johnny Thunder explores the South American rainforest) is a fascinating, heavily stylized mashup of Mesoamerican and Andean pre-Columbian cultures, but it is most directly inspired by the **Aztec** and **Inca** civilizations.

Because Lego was leaning into a pulpy, 1930s adventure-serial aesthetic, they didn't strictly stick to historical accuracy. Instead, they blended elements from a few distinct cultures into a fictionalized "Jungle" setting.

### 1. The Aztec Connection (Central Mexico)

The most prominent visual cues point toward Mesoamerica, specifically the Aztecs:

- **The Sun Disk Motif:** A central piece in several sets, like the _Amazon Ancient Ruins_, is a large, golden calendar-style disk. This heavily mimics the famous **Aztec Sun Stone** (often mislabeled as the Mayan calendar).
    
- **The Main Antagonist, Achu:** The indigenous leader/deity figure, Achu, wears a massive, brilliant feathered headdress. This is a direct nod to Mesoamerican royal regalia, highly reminiscent of the famous **Moctezuma's headdress** made of quetzal feathers.
    
- **Serpent Imagery:** The sets feature prominent snake carvings, traps, and icons, drawing heavily on the worship of the feathered serpent deity **Quetzalcoatl**.

### 2. The Inca Connection (The Andes / Peru)

While the aesthetics look Central American, Lego geographically placed the theme in South America along the Amazon River. This brought in strong Andean influences:

- **The Golden Sun God Focus:** The entire plot centers around Johnny Thunder trying to find the **"Sun Stone"** or ancient golden artifacts. The Inca were famous for their massive wealth of gold and their primary devotion to **Inti**, the Sun God.
    
- **Stepped Pyramids:** The architecture in the sets features steep, stepped stone shrines. While resembling Mayan or Aztec temples, their placement in a deep, mountainous jungle river system evokes the classic Hollywood imagery of searching for El Dorado or Machu Picchu hidden in the South American canopy.
    

### The Verdict: A Pop-Culture Blend

If you have to pin it down to one, the visual design language—the headdresses, the specific style of the stone carvings, and the calendar discs—is overwhelmingly **Aztec and Maya**.

Lego took these Mesoamerican aesthetics, transplanted them into the South American Amazon basin, seasoned it with Inca gold-hunting lore, and viewed it all through an Indiana Jones-style lens.
